A study was conducted to evaluate the dynamical stability of crystal structures generated by various models. Eight generative models were used to create over 200,000 novel structures, which were then filtered and relaxed to obtain 108,843 successfully converged crystals. Phonon-spectrum calculations were performed on these crystals, revealing that approximately 25% of them were dynamically stable. The top-performing models, including MatterGen, InvDesFlow-AL, and CrystalFormer, achieved stability ratios of 41%, 38%, and 34%, respectively. These models had been pre-trained on large, high-quality datasets, which likely contributed to their superior performance. In contrast, models trained on smaller datasets, such as CrystalFlow, exhibited significantly lower stability ratios. The results highlight the challenges of achieving dynamical stability in crystal generation and the importance of pre-training on large, high-quality datasets.
