This research explores the concept of language-guided robotic grasping, where robots use natural language instructions to accurately localize and grasp objects in cluttered environments. The proposed framework, LGGD, integrates visual and linguistic information throughout the grasp detection pipeline, allowing for more precise alignment between language intent and visual features. The model uses a combination of techniques, including dual-path cross-attention, language-conditioned upsampling, and a text-guided decoder, to refine spatial-semantic alignment and enhance grasp robustness. Experimental evaluations demonstrate that LGGD outperforms previous language-guided grasping methods, showing strong generalization to unseen objects and diverse language queries. The approach has been validated on various datasets and has been successfully deployed on a real robotic platform, highlighting its potential for instruction-aware robotic systems that can collaborate with humans in complex environments.
