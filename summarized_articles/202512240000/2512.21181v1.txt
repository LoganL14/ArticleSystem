Summary:

The Fixed-Parameter-Count Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm (FPC-QAOA) is a scalable framework for variational quantum algorithms that addresses the exponential growth of parameter space with circuit depth. FPC-QAOA separates schedule optimization from circuit digitization, enabling arbitrarily deep digitized adiabatic evolutions without increasing the number of trainable parameters or classical optimization complexity.

The algorithm preserves the core structure of QAOA while recasting the ansatz as a digitized adiabatic evolution governed by three smooth schedule functions associated with the initial, problem, and auxiliary Hamiltonians. This fixed-size parameterization enables deeper digitalization without expanding the classical search space, mitigating overparameterization and optimization challenges.

FPC-QAOA was benchmarked on random MaxCut instances and the Tail Assignment Problem, achieving performance comparable to or better than standard QAOA with nearly constant classical effort and significantly fewer quantum circuit evaluations. Experiments on the IBM Kingston superconducting processor with up to 50 qubits confirmed robustness and hardware efficiency under realistic noise.
