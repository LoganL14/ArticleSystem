This research introduces the Fixed-Parameter-Count Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm, a scalable framework designed to address key limitations in variational quantum algorithms. By separating schedule optimization from circuit digitization, the algorithm enables deep digitized adiabatic evolutions without increasing the number of trainable parameters. This approach mitigates issues such as overparameterization and barren-plateau-like behaviors, leading to more stable convergence and reduced classical overhead. The algorithm has been benchmarked on various problems, including random MaxCut instances, demonstrating improved solution quality and reduced optimization effort compared to standard Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm approaches. The results position this new paradigm as a practical and hardware-efficient solution for scalable quantum optimization on near-term quantum devices.
