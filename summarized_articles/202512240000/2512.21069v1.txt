This study presents a simplified approach to quantum algorithms for solving complex chemical systems. By eliminating less effective current operators and long-range density terms, the algorithm can allocate more parameters to impactful interactions, resulting in improved accuracy. The method is tested on various molecules, including hydrogen chains, water, and nitrogen, and achieves chemical accuracy across a wide range of geometries. The algorithm's performance is also evaluated under restrictive CNOT budgets, demonstrating its ability to prepare the ground state with high fidelity even with limited resources. These findings suggest that the method provides a substantial ground state overlap, making it an excellent initial state for quantum phase estimation on future fault-tolerant quantum hardware. Overall, the study shows that well-designed local operators are sufficient and efficient for achieving chemical accuracy in quantum computations.
