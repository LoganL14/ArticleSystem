The paper proposes a method called UniTacHand to bridge the gap between human hands and robotic dexterous hands by learning a unified tactile-pose representation. This is achieved through a two-stage process, where heterogeneous tactile data from both human and robotic domains is first projected onto a 2D UV map, and then aligned into a shared latent space using contrastive learning. The resulting representation enables zero-shot or one-shot policy transfer for tactile-based manipulation tasks, allowing for the utilization of large-scale human tactile data. The approach is compared to other baselines, demonstrating its effectiveness in unifying tactile representations and enabling policy transfer. By leveraging spatial context and aligning it to an explicit morphological space, UniTacHand presents a data-efficient alternative for learning unified representations.
