Images are then represented by their aggregated appearance patterns, which are compared at test time via nearest-neighbor search. Backbone architectures have evolved from ResNet-based [3, 27, 49] to Vision Transformers (ViTs) [32, 62, 72]. Task-specific fine-tuned foundation models, in particular DINOv2 [48], achieve nowadays state-of-the-art performance [5, 29, 30]. Feature aggregation strategies have also advanced considerably, from the seminal NetVLAD [3], inspired by the handcrafted VLAD aggregation [31], to pooling-based methods such as GeM [49], MLP-based aggregations like MixVPR [2], and approaches leveraging optimal transport [30].

All these methods, however, share the fundamental limitation of extracting features from just one single view. Multiple views provide broader scene coverage and a richer variety of viewpoints, but multi-view feature aggregation has always been a challenging and relatively unexplored topic [21]. However, the recent development of the Visual Geometry Grounded Transformer (VGGT) [61], a large ViT backbone able to model 3D spatial information from multiple views, has opened new possibilities for leveraging such information in various tasks. Building on this backbone for feature extraction, we introduce in this paper UniPR-3D, the first 3D token-based VPR framework supporting both single-frame and sequence-level place matching.
