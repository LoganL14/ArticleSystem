## Group Contractions via Infinite-Dimensional Lie Theory

David Prinz ∗ , Alexander Schmeding † and Philip K. Schwartz ‡

December 20, 2025

## Abstract

Contractions are a procedure to construct a new Lie algebra out of a given one via a singular limit. Specifically, the İnönü-Wigner construction starts with a Lie algebra g with Lie subalgebra h ⊆ g and complement n . Then, the vectors in h are rescaled by a formal parameter ε ∈ R + , which effectively turns the Lie bracket [ · , · ] into a formal power series. Notably, the limit ε → 0 trivialises certain relations, such that the complement n becomes an abelian ideal. In the present article, we are not only interested in the limiting Lie algebras and groups, but also in the corresponding series expansions in ε to understand the limiting behaviour. Particularly, we are interested in how to integrate the 'power-series-expanded' Lie algebras to the Lie group level. To this end, we reformulate the above procedure using infinite-dimensional Lie algebras of analytic germs. Then, we apply their integration theory to obtain an extensive analysis of this expansion procedure. In particular, we obtain an explicit construction of the resulting Lie algebras and groups.

Keywords: Lie group contraction, Lie algebra deformation, group of analytic germs, Banach Lie group, İnönü-Wigner contraction, Lie algebra expansion, singular limit of symmetries

Applications in mathematics: The present construction allows for various new perspectives on symmetries, their deformations, and contractions within mathematics. Notably, expansions of order k +1 can be seen as deformations of the expansions of order k . In addition, expansions of order k = 1 inherit the structure of a super Lie algebra, while general finite expansions inherit the structure of a graded Lie algebra: This perspective is sketched as an outlook in our conclusion (Section 5) and will be studied in a follow-up article. In particular, we can then apply explicit integration techniques for super or graded Lie algebras to their respective super or graded Lie groups using Harish-Chandra modules , cf. [SZ05; Gav20]. Specifically, we aim to construct a derived version, which measures the respective deformations explicitly using the dual perspective of the Chevalley-Eilenberg cochain complex, cf. [Jub+22]. More generally, we plan to generalise the present constructions to Lie algebroids and Lie groupoids as well as L ∞ -algebras and L ∞ -groups, cf. [Get09].

Applications in physics: The original motivation for Lie algebra contractions comes from physics. Here, contractions play a role in any situation where two physical theories are related by a singular limit which affects in particular symmetry transformations. Most famously, this regards the Newtonian limits of General and Special Relativity to Newtonian physics: in the 'relativistic' case, the relevant groups are the Lorentz and/or Poincaré (i.e. inhomogeneous Lorentz) groups, which the singular limit 1 /c → 0, where c is the speed of light, contracts to the homogeneous and inhomogeneous Galilei groups. This contraction 'Poincaré ⇝ Galilei' was the original case considered by İnönü and Wigner [IW53]. More generally, the classification of possible kinematical groups (i.e. groups that are allowed as spacetime symmetries) by Bacry and Lévy-Leblond [BL68] has shown that all permitted kinematical groups arise via a sequence of contractions of the de Sitter and Anti-de-Sitter groups.

A fully coordinate-free geometric description of the 'Newtonian 1 /c → 0 limit' of General Relativity and Lorentzian geometry is given by so-called Newton-Cartan gravity and Galilei geometry [Car23; Tra63; Kün72; Kün76; Ehl81; Ehl19; Sch26]. In the limit, the bundle of Lorentzian orthonormal frames is replaced by the bundle of so-called Galilei frames, whose structure group is the homogeneous Galilei group-i.e., heuristically speaking, the contraction happens at the level of structure groups of bundles of adapted frames. In this context, the notion of Lie algebra expansions [KK11] has recently been used to give a coordinate-free description of the post-Newtonian expansion of General Relativity in a post-Newton-Cartan formalism, by considering connections valued in Lie algebra expansions of the Lorentz / Poincaré algebra corresponding to their contractions to the Galilei algebras [HHO19; HHO20; HOO23]. The results of the present paper open up the interesting possibility to extend this description rigorously to the level of Lie groups .

Further, it would be interesting to apply our results to the Lie groups arising as asymptotic symmetry groups of asymptotically flat spacetimes, such as the BMS(3 , 1) and NU(3 , 1) groups, cf. [PS22a; PS22b]. However, as the BMS and NU groups are semidirect products of a finitedimensional Lie group and a space of smooth functions, they are not Banach Lie groups. Even more severe, these groups are not locally exponential Lie groups (in the sense that their Lie group exponential does not provide exponential coordinates around the identity [Chi+25]), whence our methods are not applicable. However, as the space of smooth functions is a tame Fréchet space, [Ham82], the BMS group is a benign inverse limit of Banach spaces. Conceivably, the ideas developed in this article together with methods for inverse limit groups (à la Omori's treatment of the diffeomorphism group [Omo74]) will allow one to treat contractions and expansions thereof of at least the BMS group. We leave this to future work.

This article is structured as follows: Westart in Section 2 with general Lie algebra contractions and the necessary background on infinite-dimensional Lie theory: Especially, this includes Lie algebras and Lie groups of germs of real-analytic mappings. Then, we proceed in Section 3 by

reformulating Lie algebra contractions and expansions in terms of germ algebras: Notably, in Proposition 3.2 we identify the contracted Lie algebra with the zero-order expansion defined via analytic germs. Furthermore, in Remark 3.4, we discuss the meaning of the corresponding higherorder expansions. Next, in Section 4, we study the corresponding Lie group level: Specifically, this includes our main result Theorem 4.4, which lifts all constructions performed previously on the level of germ Lie algebras to the corresponding germ Lie groups. Moreover, in Theorem 4.8, we use this to explicitly identify the Lie group expansions as specific Banach Lie groups. Finally, in Section 5, we conclude our findings and give an outlook to our planned follow-up project: Interestingly, some of the present constructions can be obtained in a more explicit way using super and graded Lie theory and Harish-Chandra modules . We believe that this perspective, using a different language, provides a valuable addition to our present results.

## 2.2 Elements of infinite-dimensional Lie theory

Here, we recall general results on subgroups and quotients of locally exponential Lie groups (which may be infinite-dimensional) from [Nee06]. Locally exponential Lie groups are Lie groups for which the Lie group exponential induces a local diffeomorphism around the identity. Even more restrictive, the class of Baker-Campbell-Hausdorff (BCH) Lie groups requires that the BCH series converges locally in the Lie algebra and yields an analytic model of the group multiplication. For these Lie groups, many basic constructions from finite-dimensional Lie theory carry over to the infinite dimensional setting.

Definition 2.8 ([Nee06], Definition IV.3.2) . Let G be a locally exponential Lie group. A closed subgroup H ⊆ G is a (locally exponential) Lie subgroup of G if it is a locally exponential Lie group with respect to the induced topology. 5

## 5 Conclusion and outlook

We reviewed general Lie algebra contractions and the particular case of the İnönü-Wigner procedure. Then, we integrated the latter setup to the corresponding Lie group level using infinite-dimensional Lie theory: Specifically, we reformulated the corresponding setup using Lie algebras of analytic germs in Proposition 3.2 and Remark 3.4. Next, using their integration theory, we obtained our main results: First, we lifted the construction of Lie algebra expansions via germs to the Lie group

level in Theorem 4.4, and then explicitly identified the expansion groups as Banach Lie groups in Theorem 4.8. We exemplified our findings with the following standard example: In the beginning of Section 1, we discussed the Lie algebra contraction so (3) ⇝ iso (2). Then, in Example 4.10, we explicitly calculated the respective first-order Lie group expansion of SO(3) ⇝ ISO(2).

In a direct follow-up article, we plan to establish an additional perspective on Lie algebra and Lie group contractions using super and graded Lie theory: Explicitly, Lie algebra expansions of order k = 1 inherit the structure of super Lie algebras, while expansions for finite k ∈ N ≥ 2 inherit the structure of graded-nilpotent Lie algebras. Notably, super and graded-nilpotent Lie algebras can be integrated using so-called Harish-Chandra modules , cf. [SZ05; Gav20]: This corresponds essentially to the semidirect product structure we found in Equation (4.21).

Furthermore, we plan to obtain a derived version using the dual perspective of the ChevalleyEilenberg cochain complex, cf. [Jub+22]. Moreover, we want to obtain a similar construction for Lie algebroids and Lie groupoids, as well as L ∞ -algebras and L ∞ -groups, cf. [Get09].

Finally, we want to obtain the Lie group contraction for a general Lie algebra contraction map.
